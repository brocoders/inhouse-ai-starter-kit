A starting point. Change what is wrong for you and hand it to the agent as the
brief — see [how to use a recipe](./index.mdx).

**For whom:** a team of five to thirty who have tried three task products and
abandoned all of them, because the work has a shape — a job, a site visit, a
case, a batch — that none of them has.
**Why now:** "Everything is in a chat thread, and on Monday I cannot say what is
behind and why."

## What a person can do

- See what is on them today, on a phone, without filtering anything.
- Create a piece of work: what it is, who it is for, who is doing it, when it is
  due.
- Move it through the steps your team actually uses, and say in a line what
  happened at each step.
- Hand it to somebody else, and have them told.
- See everything that is late, and everything with nobody on it.
- Look back at a finished piece of work and see its whole path.

## Screens

- **Mine** — the opening screen: what is assigned to you, overdue first, then due
  today, then the rest. One column on a phone.
- **The board** — everything by step, with counts, filtered by person and by
  whom it is for. Filters live in the address so a view can be sent.
- **A piece of work** — its own page: what, for whom, who is on it, the step, the
  due date, and the whole history of steps and notes.
- **New** — a short form; everything except the title has a sensible default.
- **Late and unassigned** — the two lists a lead actually needs on a Monday.

## Records

- **Work item** — title, description, who it is for, who is on it, step, due
  date, priority, when it was finished.
- **Step change** — item, from, to, who, when, note.
- **Note** — item, who, when, what.

Steps are a short fixed list to begin with — to do, doing, waiting on somebody,
done, dropped — named in your team's own words. Naming them is the single most
useful hour you will spend on this, because everything else follows from it.

## Rules

- Every item has exactly one person on it, or is explicitly unassigned and shows
  up on the unassigned list. "Everyone's job" is how things are missed.
- Moving to **waiting on somebody** requires a line saying who and for what.
- Moving to **dropped** requires a reason, and the item stays with its history
  rather than being deleted.
- "Late" is anything past its due date that is not finished, counted in your own
  time zone.
- Reassigning an item notifies the person it went to, once, and records the
  handover.
- An item finished is not editable except for its notes, so a completed record
  stays true.

## Done when

1. You open the app on a phone and see only what is on you, overdue at the top.
2. You create an item in under thirty seconds with a title and a due date.
3. You move it to **waiting on somebody**, are asked who and why, and both appear
   on its history.
4. You hand it to a colleague and they are told once.
5. Something due yesterday and unfinished appears on the **late** list.
6. You filter the board to one person and one step, send the address to a
   colleague, and they see the same board.
7. You drop an item with a reason; it leaves the board and is still findable with
   its whole path intact.

## Out of scope

Sub-tasks, dependencies and anything resembling a project plan. Time tracking
and estimates. Recurring work — genuinely useful, and worth adding once you know
which of your steps repeat. Attachments beyond what every record already
supports. Sprints, points and velocity: if the team wants those, a product that
does them well already exists.
